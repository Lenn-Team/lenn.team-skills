# Craft

## 1. Prosody – stress and beat
- Stressed syllables on strong beats (in 4/4 beats 1 and 3); stress content words, not articles and prepositions.
- Never shift the stress of a multisyllabic word to make a line fit ("to-MOR-row" stays, "TO-mor-row" is an error, not a style).
- Test: **speak** the lyrics aloud, then speak them rhythmically at target tempo. Where you stumble, the singing stumbles.
- Without a melody: check syllable count and stress pattern, mark the musical fit as open.

## 2. Rhyme

**Types:** perfect rhyme (time/rhyme; Zeit/weit), near/slant rhyme (time/mind), assonance (same vowels), consonance (same final consonants), internal rhyme, multisyllabic rhyme (rap).

**Schemes and effect:**

| Scheme | Effect |
|---|---|
| AABB | stable, immediate resolution, childlike/anthemic |
| ABAB | classic, delayed resolution |
| XAXA / ABCB | lighter, less pressure, more natural tone |
| AAAX | build-up, surprising ending |
| AAB | blues |

**Default by genre:** rap AABB (strict, multisyllabic), pop XAXA (loose), rock XAXA/ABAB, punk loose AABB, folk/country ABCB, Schlager AABB/ABAB (perfect rhyme), EDM repetition instead of rhyme, jazz AABA (demanding).

**Rules:**
- Judge rhymes by sound (from the last stressed vowel on), not by spelling.
- No word rhymes with itself; no repeated end word in consecutive lines.
- Meaning before rhyme: a near rhyme with an apt statement beats a perfect rhyme with a filler word.
- Prediction test: cover the rhyme word. If you can guess it, find another (exception: deliberately fulfilled expectation, see `impact.md`).
- Scheme consistent within a section; no orphan lines.

## 3. Show, don't tell
- **Action:** what does someone do out of the feeling? ("She sets out two cups, then puts one back." instead of "She is lonely.")
- **Objects:** visible, tangible nouns.
- **Senses:** more than sight – sound, smell, temperature, touch.
- Division of labour: verses = scene and sensory detail; chorus = emotional statement, condensed.

## 4. Verse, chorus, bridge
- **Verse:** observing, narrative, specific, more syllables.
- **Pre-chorus:** tension, often shorter lines, rising towards the chorus.
- **Chorus:** emotional, more universal, shorter, the title.
- **Bridge:** contrast (perspective, time, realisation) – afterwards the chorus reads differently.
- **No echo:** the verse does not anticipate words, images and rhyme words of the chorus. It hints, the chorus names.
- **No twin verses:** verse 2 drives forward (new information, complication, time jump, change of perspective).

## 5. Hook and title
- Title short and memorable, belongs in the first or last chorus line and appears early.
- A hook lives on rhythm and sound: short words, open vowels on long notes, one peculiarity.
- Title not an abstract noun ("Freedom", "Echoes"), but a phrase, image or turn of phrase people want to repeat.

## 6. Length and density

**Duration → word count:**

| Target duration | Non-rap | Rap |
|---|---|---|
| 2:00–2:30 | 120–180 | 200–300 |
| 2:30–3:30 | 150–250 | 250–400 |
| 3:30–5:00 | 220–400 | 400–600 |
| 5:00–7:00 | 350–500 | 550–750 |

Plan about 30 words fewer per instrumental section. Over 400 words (rap 600): cut, unless the target duration is above 5:00; under 150 words check whether sections are missing (except in electronic genres).

**Lines per section (max):**

| Genre | Verse | Chorus | Bridge | Pre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rap | 8 (per block) | 4–8 | 4–6 | 2–4 |
| Pop / Schlager / rock / indie | 6–8 | 4–6 | 4 | 2–4 |
| Punk | 4–6 | 2–4 | 2–4 | 2 |
| Metal | 4–8 | 4–6 | 4 | 2–4 |
| Folk / country / singer-songwriter | 4–8 | 4–6 | 2–4 | 2–4 |
| Electronic | 4–6 | 2–4 | 2–4 | – |
| Lo-fi / ambient | 2–4 | 2–4 | 2 | – |
| R&B / soul | 6–8 | 4–6 | 4 | 2–4 |
| Ballad | 4–6 | 4–6 | 2–4 | – |

**Density by tempo:**

| BPM | max lines/verse | topics/verse |
|---|---|---|
| < 80 | 4 | 1–2 |
| 80–94 | 4–6 | 1–2 |
| 94–110 | 6 | 2–3 |
| 110–140 | 6–8 | 2–3 |
| > 140 | 4 | 1 |

Two 4-line verses are better than one stuffed 8-line verse. At most three proper names per verse. Every topic needs at least one pair of lines.

**Syllables per line (guide):** pop/folk/punk 6–8, rock/indie 8–10, rap 10–13+, chorus rather shorter than verse. Parallel lines ±2.

## 7. Point of view and tense
Keep I/you/we/he-she consistent; keep tense consistent. Change only as a deliberate effect (e.g. bridge in present tense after a memory in past tense).

## 8. Typical mistakes
- Forced stress, twisted word order for the rhyme, worn-out rhyme pairs
- Filler words for syllable count ("so", "just", "oh yeah"; German "ja", "doch")
- Too vague (fits everything) or too specific (in-jokes no listener can access)
- No recognisable hook, insincere voice
- Invented contractions ("signal'd") – only standard contractions (I'd, wouldn't; German "hab", "gibt's")
- Verse too dense for the tempo

## 9. Occasion and commissioned songs
- Use only facts provided, names exact, clarify pronunciation.
- Dose in-jokes: one or two references for insiders, the rest must be understandable for guests.
- For organisations and clubs: ask about values and self-image, avoid ad language – a specific scene from club life rather than "we are strong, we stick together".
