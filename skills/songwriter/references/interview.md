# Interview

The goal of the interview is material, not a completed form. The best lines of a song almost always come from a detail the user mentions in passing. So follow up when something specific flashes up ("What colour was the car?", "What exactly did he say?").

Ask the questions in the user's language.

## Procedure

1. **Round 1 – Core** (always): what is it about, for whom, what for, which language, which direction.
2. **Round 2 – Story**: situation, characters, details, turning point.
3. **Round 3 – Music**: genre, tempo/energy, voice, references, instruments.
4. **Round 4 – Frame** (only if needed): length, platform, release, taboos.

At most 3–5 questions per round. Don't ask what can reasonably be assumed – mark the assumption in the brief. Someone who says "surprise me" gets a proposal with reasons instead of more questions.

Closed questions (genre, tempo, voice, generator, trend stance) work well as clickable choices. Open questions about stories and details stay free text.

## Question bank

### Core
- What should it be about – in one sentence? What should someone feel or do when the song ends?
- Who is the song for? (yourself, a person, an audience, an occasion, a brand/organisation)
- What for? (streaming release, gift, party, jingle, anthem for a club, video, fun)
- Language(s)? Dialect or standard language? Mixed?
- Is there anything already – a line, a title, a melody, a voice memo?

### Story and material
- What actually happened? When, where, with whom?
- Which objects, places, smells, sounds belong to it?
- A sentence someone really said?
- Where does the story tip – what is different before and after? (emotional turn)
- From whose point of view? I, you, we, observer?
- What must not appear under any circumstances? (names, topics, words, clichés)
- For occasion songs: names and their pronunciation, nicknames, shared in-jokes, what is true and what may be exaggerated?

### Music
- Which direction? Genre(s), or: "Where should the song play?" (car at night, festival tent, club, campfire, church, headphones on the train)
- Energy and tempo: calm, medium, driving? Danceable?
- Voice: female, male, duet, choir, rap? Colour (raspy, breathy, powerful, clear, young, mature)?
- Two or three reference songs – and **what exactly** you like about them (groove, voice, chorus, sound, mood)? Optionally upload an audio file (`audio-references.md`).
- Instruments that must be there or must not?
- Follow the current trend, deliberately go against it, or timeless? (`trends.md`)

### Frame
- Target generator: Suno, ElevenLabs, both for comparison, real musicians?
- Target length? (default: 2:45–3:30 for streaming pop, otherwise by genre)
- Release planned? If so: artist name, platforms, timeframe.
- Explicit language okay?

## From interview to brief

Don't write lyrics straight after the interview. First fill in the song brief (`../templates/song-brief.md`), mark assumptions and have it confirmed. A brief corrected in five minutes saves three rounds of revising lyrics.
