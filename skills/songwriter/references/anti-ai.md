# Human, not generic

Language models write what is most likely to come next. That is exactly what sounds like AI: the expected image, the expected rhyme, the expected moral. Music generators amplify it – Suno demonstrably favours words like *neon, echo, ghost, silver, shadow, whisper, crystal, velvet*. This file is the antidote. Apply it **before every lyric output**.

The lists are not an absolute ban. A listed word is allowed if it is set deliberately, anchored concretely and justified in the brief or check notes (e.g. Schlager using familiarity on purpose; irony). Unjustified: replace it.

## 1. The four tests

1. **Swap test:** could the line appear in any other song? → replace.
2. **Pub test:** would a person say it like this when telling a friend about it? If not, it's fake poetry.
3. **Prediction test:** cover the rhyme word or image. If you can guess it, it's too likely.
4. **Specificity test:** at least one detail per verse that belongs only to this story (object, place, time, the make of the car, a sentence someone actually said).

## 4. Patterns that sound like AI

- **Every line a metaphor.** People also write plain, literal lines. Rule of thumb: at most every third line figurative.
- **Image soup:** fire, ocean, stars and storm in the same song. One image field per song, two at most.
- **Abstract chorus without an anchor:** "we are free, we are one, we are more" – the chorus may be more general than the verse but needs one tangible element.
- **Chains of three adjectives** ("wild, free and untamed").
- **Perfect symmetry everywhere:** all lines the same length, all rhymes perfect, all verses 4 lines. A deliberately shorter line, a near rhyme, a pause sound human.
- **The moral at the end:** "and I have learned …" – listeners prefer to draw conclusions themselves.
- **Naming the feeling instead of showing it** ("I'm so sad", "my heart is breaking").
- **Generic "you":** a "you" without any characteristic.
- **Empowerment formulas** without a story ("I'm a fighter").
- **Filler interjections** ("oh-oh", "yeah", "na-na") without a melodic function.
- **Cosmic inflation:** universe, galaxy, infinity for everyday feelings.
- **Explained punchline:** the bridge explains what the chorus already showed.

## 5. What sounds human

- Specific objects, places, times, weather, sounds, smells.
- Direct speech and snippets of conversation.
- Everyday language and the narrator's own idiom (dialect, slang, jargon – fitting the character, not mixed).
- Contradiction and ambivalence ("I don't miss you. Just your dog.").
- Humour, self-irony, understatement.
- Strong, specific verbs instead of verb + adverb ("shuffle" instead of "walk slowly").
- A break in the pattern at the right place.
- Numbers and names (sparingly, pronounceable).
- Something imperfect that may stay because it sounds true.

## 6. How to replace
1. What should the line do? (information, feeling, transition, rhyme)
2. Which detail from the interview fits?
3. Write the new line, then find the rhyme anew – not the other way round.
4. If there is no detail: ask the user instead of inventing ("What was on your kitchen table?").

## 7. Prompts too
Keep style prompts free of empty phrases as well: "epic, emotional, powerful, amazing" say nothing. Use concrete sound description instead (instrument, playing style, room, era, vocal texture). See `suno.md`.
