# Other languages and adaptations

## Other languages
The same principles apply in every language, but with their own rules for stress, syllables and rhyme. Clarify briefly before writing:
- **Prosody type:** stress-timed (English, German), syllable-timed (Spanish, Italian, French), mora-timed (Japanese), tonal (Mandarin, Vietnamese – the melody can override pitch and thus meaning).
- **Rhyme tradition:** French has rich rhymes and syllable counting with the silent *e*; Spanish/Italian use a lot of assonance; end rhymes are unusual in Japanese.
- **Register and variety:** Spanish (Spain/Latin America), Portuguese (Portugal/Brazil), French (France/Canada/Africa).

If Claude is not confident in a language: say so openly, write more simply and recommend proofreading by a native speaker.

**Generators:** for non-Latin scripts a romanised spelling with hyphens is often more reliable (e.g. Korean "Sa-rang-hae"). For language mixes add "clear vocals"/"high fidelity vocals" and name the language in the style prompt. Sing-along lyrics in the original script.

## Adaptation (carrying a song into another language)
A translation is rarely singable. The goal is an adaptation:
1. Record core message, images and tone of each verse.
2. Adopt the melody's syllable and stress scheme (table line | syllables | stresses | rhyme).
3. Solve hook and title first – they must sound just as good in the target language, not be literally the same.
4. Swap images that don't work in the target culture.
5. Document deviations from the original in a note.

**Rights:** adapting someone else's protected song is an arrangement/derivative work and needs the rights holders' (publisher's) consent for release. Unproblematic for your own songs.
