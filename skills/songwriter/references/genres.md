# Genre profiles

Reference values for the brief and the style prompt. They describe what is typical, not what is mandatory – deliberate deviations are fine if they are in the brief. Tempo in BPM; for halftime genres the felt tempo is half.

Lyric legend: **lines/verse** (typical–max), **syllables/line**, **rhyme** (scheme, strictness).

## Pop
- **Tempo:** 90–128 (pop ballads 70–90)
- **Instruments:** synths, piano, acoustic/electric guitar, programmed drums, sub-bass
- **Vocals:** close, clear, doubles in the chorus, harmonies; conversational tone
- **Production:** modern, polished, sidechain-pumping bass possible, dry verse / wide chorus
- **Form:** V–Pre–C–V–Pre–C–B–C, post-chorus hooks common
- **Lyrics:** 4–8 lines/verse, 6–10 syllables, XAXA, near rhymes fine; precise perspective, one strong hook
- **Word budget:** 250–350 words at 3:00–4:00
- **German pop (Deutschpop):** speech-like, conversational, close perspective; see `languages/german.md`

## Schlager / Discofox (German-language party pop)
- **Tempo:** 120–132 (Discofox), Schlager ballad 70–90
- **Instruments:** four-on-the-floor drums, synth bass, pads, guitar licks, brass/string samples
- **Vocals:** direct, clear, made for singing along, often duet or choir in the chorus
- **Production:** bright, loud, catchy; key change before the last chorus is classic
- **Form:** V–C–V–C–B–C–C (chorus doubled at the end)
- **Lyrics:** 4–8 lines, 7–10 syllables, AABB or ABAB, perfect rhymes preferred; direct address ("du"), familiar phrases used deliberately, but one original punchline per song
- **Note:** familiarity is part of the genre – don't apply the anti-AI list rigidly, check for interchangeability instead.

## Singer-songwriter / Folk
- **Tempo:** 70–120
- **Instruments:** acoustic guitar (fingerpicking/strumming), piano, upright bass, light percussion, mandolin, banjo, accordion
- **Vocals:** intimate, narrative, few effects
- **Production:** natural, room sound, little compression
- **Form:** strophic with refrain or AABA; narrative arc over 3 verses
- **Lyrics:** 6–8 lines, 7–10 syllables, ABCB, medium rhyme strictness; story, details, punchline at the end
- **Word budget:** 250–400

## Indie / Alternative
- **Tempo:** 90–140
- **Instruments:** guitars with character (chorus, fuzz, clean), bass, live drums, vintage synths
- **Vocals:** imperfection allowed, idiosyncratic phrasing
- **Production:** lo-fi to hi-fi, room, band feel
- **Lyrics:** 6–8 lines, 8–10 syllables, loose XAXA; unusual images, irony, breaks welcome

## Rock / Alternative rock
- **Tempo:** 100–150
- **Instruments:** distorted electric guitars, bass, live drums, possibly organ
- **Vocals:** powerful, raspy, belting in the chorus
- **Form:** V–C–V–C–solo/B–C
- **Lyrics:** 6–8 lines, 8–10 syllables, XAXA or ABAB
- **Suno note:** heavy guitars sometimes sound washed out, vocals drift towards generic post-grunge – describe the voice very specifically, name the era.

## Punk / Pop-punk
- **Tempo:** 160–200 (pop-punk 150–180)
- **Instruments:** fast power chords, driving bass, snare on every beat
- **Vocals:** direct, shouted, gang vocals in the chorus
- **Lyrics:** 4–6 lines, 6–8 syllables, loose AABB; short, angry or ironic, a slogan as hook
- **Word budget:** 150–250 at 2:30–3:00

## Metal
- **Tempo:** 90–200 (doom 60–80)
- **Instruments:** down-tuned guitars, double bass drum, possibly orchestra/synths
- **Vocals:** clean, shouts or growls – specify exactly
- **Lyrics:** 4–8 lines, variable, rhyme optional; breakdown 2–4 lines
- **Suno note:** niche subgenres often miss; main genre + one modifier.

## Hip-hop / Rap
- **Boom bap:** 85–95, sampled drums, vinyl crackle, jazz/soul samples
- **Trap:** 130–150 (halftime feel), 808 bass, hi-hat rolls
- **Drill:** 140–145, sliding 808s, syncopated snares
- **Cloud / melodic rap:** 70–90 or 130–150, pads, auto-tune as effect
- **Vocals:** describe the flow (laid-back, double-time, melodic), ad-libs
- **Form:** hook (8 lines) – verse (16 bars ≈ 16 lines, split into blocks of 8 for Suno) – hook
- **Lyrics:** 8 lines per block, 10–13+ syllables, AABB, high rhyme strictness: multisyllabic and internal rhymes, punchlines, pauses
- **Word budget:** 400–600

## R&B / Soul
- **Tempo:** 60–100
- **Instruments:** Rhodes/electric piano, warm bass, soft drums, guitar licks, strings
- **Vocals:** melismas, falsetto, harmonies, ad-libs
- **Lyrics:** 6–8 lines, flexible, near rhymes, sensual and specific; leave room for held notes

## Electronic: house / EDM / techno / drum & bass
- **House:** 120–128; **EDM/big room:** 126–130; **Techno:** 125–140; **Drum & bass:** 170–176; **UK garage:** 130–135
- **Instruments:** synths (supersaw, pluck, acid 303), four-on-the-floor or breakbeats, sub-bass
- **Vocals:** short phrases, vocal chops, often one single repeated line
- **Form:** intro – build – drop – break – build – drop – outro
- **Lyrics:** 2–4 lines per section, repetition instead of rhyme; drop instrumental (tag, no lyrics)
- **Word budget:** 80–250

## Country
- **Tempo:** 80–120
- **Instruments:** acoustic and Telecaster guitar, pedal steel, fiddle, banjo
- **Vocals:** narrative, light twang
- **Lyrics:** 6–8 lines, ABCB, story with a twist in the last chorus (meaning flips)

## Ballad (across genres)
- **Tempo:** 60–80
- **Instruments:** piano or guitar, strings later
- **Lyrics:** 4–6 lines, room to breathe, restraint instead of a metaphor per line
- **Word budget:** 180–280

## Lo-fi / Chill / Ambient
- **Tempo:** 60–90
- **Instruments:** muffled keys, vinyl noise, soft drums, pads
- **Lyrics:** 0–4 lines per section, lots of instrumental space

## Reggae / Dancehall / Afrobeats
- **Reggae:** 60–90 (offbeat guitar, one drop); **Dancehall:** 90–105; **Afrobeats:** 95–115 (log drum, percussion, synth plucks)
- **Lyrics:** 4–8 lines, call-and-response, riddim-driven; patois/pidgin only if the user wants it and it fits

## Jazz / Swing / Chanson
- **Tempo:** 60–200, swing feel
- **Instruments:** upright bass, brushed drums, piano, horns
- **Lyrics:** AABA (32 bars), demanding rhymes, wordplay

## Folk-traditional / Shanty / Club anthem / Stadium song
- **Tempo:** 90–130 (shanty often 100–120, stomp feel)
- **Instruments:** accordion, stomps and claps, choir, brass
- **Vocals:** group singing, lead singer – choir
- **Lyrics:** short, shoutable lines, perfect rhyme, chorus as slogan, local reference; very high repetition wanted

## Genre blends
Combine at most two, at most three genres, and name one as the main genre. Extreme fusions get muddy in generators. Better: main genre + instrument/production marker from the second genre ("Schlager pop with Afrobeats percussion").

## Generator quality by genre (research status Nov 2026, Suno v6)
Good: pop, electronic (e.g. drum & bass, UK garage), hip-hop, singer-songwriter. Weaker: rock, metal, grunge, blues rock, alt-country, niche subgenres. For weak genres note the expectation in the brief and plan more generations. Check the current state before use – models change.
